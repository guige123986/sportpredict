Major League Baseball (MLB) is the oldest professional sports league in the United States, founded in 1903 with roots tracing back to the 1860s. It features 30 teams split between the American League and National League, each playing 162 regular-season games from late March through September. With up to 15 games daily, MLB offers the highest volume of betting opportunities of any major sport, and the marathon season rewards patience and disciplined analysis over emotional reactions to short-term results. The playoff structure features 12 teams 鈥?six from each league 鈥?with division winners and wild card entries competing through the Division Series, Championship Series, and ultimately the World Series in late October. Iconic franchises like the New York Yankees, Boston Red Sox, Los Angeles Dodgers, and Chicago Cubs carry enormous historical weight, while recent seasons have seen small-market teams like the Texas Rangers and Tampa Bay Rays demonstrate that smart roster construction can overcome financial limitations. MLB betting is distinct from other sports due to the overwhelming importance of starting pitchers. A team's odds can shift dramatically based on who takes the mound, making starting pitcher analysis the single most important factor in daily baseball betting. Other key variables include ballpark dimensions (Coors Field in Denver and Fenway Park in Boston create unique scoring environments), weather conditions (wind direction at Wrigley Field, humidity in Arlington), travel schedules, and bullpen availability. The sheer volume of games means bookmakers occasionally misprice matchups, particularly in day games after night games and during long road trips.
