Bookmaker Margin & Overround Explained

What Is Overround?

Overround (also known as the "book," "vig," "juice," or "margin") is the built-in advantage that bookmakers embed into their odds to guarantee a profit regardless of the outcome. In a perfectly fair market, the implied probabilities of all possible outcomes would sum to exactly 100%. However, bookmakers price their odds so that these probabilities add up to more than 100% — typically between 102% and 110% for major markets — and the excess over 100% represents the overround.

For example, in a tennis match where both players are evenly matched, fair odds would be 2.00 on each player (50% implied probability each, totaling 100%). But a bookmaker might offer 1.90 on each player, implying a 52.6% probability for each outcome. The total implied probability is 105.3%, meaning the overround is 5.3%. This 5.3% is the bookmaker's theoretical profit margin on this market.

Understanding overround is essential because it is the single biggest factor working against bettors. To profit from sports betting, you need to overcome this margin first before you can even begin to gain an edge. A bettor who is right 55% of the time on even-money bets is still losing money if the overround exceeds 10%.

How Bookmakers Build Margin

Bookmakers construct their odds through a multi-step process. First, their analysts and models estimate the true probability of each outcome. Then, they apply a margin to these probabilities to ensure profitability. The margin is distributed across the outcomes — it is not always spread evenly.

In practice, bookmakers often apply more margin to outcomes that attract heavy public betting (such as favorites in football) and less margin to less popular outcomes. This is because public bettors tend to back favorites regardless of value, allowing bookmakers to charge a higher effective margin on these selections. Conversely, sharp bettors tend to bet on underdogs when value exists, so bookmakers keep margins tighter there to remain competitive.

The margin also varies by market and sport. Major markets like Premier League match outcomes typically have margins of 2-5%, while niche markets like lower-league player props can carry margins of 8-15%. Exchange-based platforms generally offer lower margins than traditional bookmakers because they earn through commissions rather than built-in margins.

Calculating True Odds

To calculate the true (fair) odds, you need to remove the bookmaker's margin from the offered odds. This process is called "removing the overround." The simplest method is to divide each implied probability by the total implied probability (the sum of all outcome probabilities including overround).

For a football match with Home odds 1.80 (55.6%), Draw odds 3.60 (27.8%), and Away odds 4.50 (22.2%), the total implied probability is 105.6%. To find the true probability: Home = 55.6% / 105.6% = 52.7%, Draw = 27.8% / 105.6% = 26.3%, Away = 22.2% / 105.6% = 21.0%. The true (fair) decimal odds would then be: Home = 1.90, Draw = 3.80, Away = 4.76. Our Odds Calculator performs these calculations automatically.

Why Margins Matter

Bookmaker margins directly impact your long-term profitability. A 2% margin means you need to be at least 2% better than the bookmaker's estimates to break even, while a 5% margin means you need to be 5% better. Over hundreds or thousands of bets, this difference compounds enormously.

Consider a bettor who places 1,000 bets per year at $100 each with a 54% win rate on even-money selections. At a 2% margin bookmaker, they are profitable. At a 5% margin bookmaker, they are losing money — despite being right more often than they are wrong. This is why consistently betting at the best available odds across multiple bookmakers is one of the most impactful things any bettor can do.
